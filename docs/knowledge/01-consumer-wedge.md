# The consumer wedge

**Decision (2026-09-22):** Wispr Flow is enterprise. Granola is prosumer. The wedge is everyday consumer — an individual-centered context graph of the human, not a business tool.

## The wedge use cases

Everyday moments for the average person, not the enterprise buyer:

- **Doctor's appointments** with summarized notes — you never remember what the doctor said; the buddy does.
- **An afternoon walk with a friend** — the conversation is captured, the follow-ups surface later.
- **Afternoon conversations, voice memos, late-night rambles** — ambient life context that compounds.

This is Pincus and Tan's own framing: the $10–100B company nobody's built is the *consumer* always-on companion, not a better work meeting tool.

## Why the lane is empty

Everyone with money is fighting over the work meeting. The consumer ambient *utility* lane has no incumbent:

- **Friend** tried a consumer companion and died (~3,000 units; ~$348K revenue vs ~$1M marketing; subway ads defaced as a "surveillance tool"; Wired: "incredibly antisocial") — because it was companionship *without utility*. See [04-always-on-space.md](04-always-on-space.md).
- **Limitless → Meta, Bee → Amazon:** absorbed, products buried. **Humane** dead. **Rabbit** 95% daily-user dropout. **Omi** niche.
- **Plaud** (1.5M+ devices) is a press-to-record recorder — the only traction story — not an always-on buddy.
- No shipping consumer product interjects usefully in live conversation. The infrastructure just arrived (GPT-Live-1, TML full-duplex); the judgment layer doesn't exist. That's ours to build.

## Why it compounds

Every capture enriches a personal graph — a moat per-meeting tools structurally can't build:

- Granola/Wispr's value is per-meeting; ours grows with the life. The longer you use it, the more *yours* it is — switching costs become the graph itself.
- The graph is **user-owned**, modeled as *claims with provenance* (transcript moment → claim → follow-up), not black-box summaries. This is Garry Tan's gbrain pattern: 155,795 pages of his life, recorder summaries treated as claims not facts. Our privacy posture makes it shippable.

## The two watch-outs

1. **Consent must be graduated.** Friend's "wearing a wire" lesson is real: ambient capture reads as surveillance jewelry to bystanders. Start with consent-legible moments (doctor's visits where everyone benefits, your own walks) before socially trickier ones (walk with a friend). Consent UX: explicit → tap-to-confirm → ambient, per relationship — not one global toggle. Regulatory direction is unambiguous: FTC fined Cox Media Group $930K over fake "Active Listening" consent claims; 86% of consumers demand AI disclosure; Granola faces a wiretap class action (see [02-granola.md](02-granola.md)).
2. **Willingness to pay concentrates in high-anxiety moments.** Medical recall is a killer wedge feature: nobody remembers what the doctor said, and the anxiety is real. General conversation capture is the habit; the doctor's visit is the hook.

## The thesis check

- **Not ownable by an incumbent:** Wispr/Granola are structurally pointed at work; Meta/Amazon absorbed the hardware plays. Nobody owns the consumer context graph.
- **Surfs model progress:** cheaper intelligence (Tan's Jevons paradox) makes the always-on buddy cheaper and more ambitious over time — the market grows into us.
- **gbrain validates the instinct:** Garry Tan's entire system is an individual context graph. The demand side is real.
